## Stage 1 Visual Target

Stage 1 proves the character design plus a simple idle/walk read, not full animation polish. Use the reference turnaround sheet to keep the four directions consistent. Then create one coherent `8x4` final state sheet and split it into 32 isolated character-only frames: 2 idle frames plus 6 simple walk-loop frames for down, up, left, and right. The two idle frames are `normal -> little 1-3px bob/squish`, returning to normal in the runtime loop. The bob/squish must be present in the grouped state sheet itself; do not create it after split by shifting, squashing, stretching, repainting, or otherwise changing frame pose/silhouette. Each direction must have a non-duplicated idle pair and at least 3 unique walk frames in the composed sheet. The validator rejects idle frame 1 if it squats too deeply by losing too much visible height, moving its alpha center too far, or dropping the cap/body too low.

Thalla must read at `64px` on mobile as an ancient gold-white mushroom-elf sovereign with visible mushroom-elf biology, strong simple silhouette, grounded feet/base over the shared separate chibi shadow layer, dark warm outline, and restrained bone/gold/white/brown palette. Quality bar: in the composed field preview, the chibi must look at least as crisp, contrasted, and finished as the approved bushes, mushrooms, and gates. Avoid a blurry/downscaled sticker look; use chunky readable shapes, a thicker warm-brown outline, clear face/cap/robe value separation, and a stronger tiny silhouette. Use `docs/home-field-chibi-style-reference.md` for the desired squat field-sprite feel and BJD-inspired chibi doll simplicity, including the liked 2026-06-23 Thalla reference as a positive face/cap/robe direction, but do not copy any reference image and do not use huge white portrait eyes. The rejected beige/pixel-art Thalla candidate is a negative example because it is too generic; the 2026-06-20 Stage 1 candidate is also a negative example because it is still too ornate and not simple/doll-like enough. A soft beige low-contrast chibi is also a negative result even when it passes alpha/readability.

Do not treat mechanical success as style success. Passing sheet mapping, alpha, dimensions, and mobile readability only means the file is usable evidence. It is still a failed chibi candidate if the composed clean preview does not match the hand-drawn elevated 2.5D field-sprite style or if the raw source, alpha edge safety, anchor/footing, separate-shadow policy, or runtime-scale read fails the runtime asset contract. Do not create or commit deterministic/mechanical fallback chibi art unless the user explicitly asks for diagnostic scaffolding; it must never be reported as a fresh imagegen art candidate.

Use an elevated top-down 2.5D hub-sprite view: the top of the mushroom cap/head should be visible, the body should feel planted on the map, and the pose should feel like a small walkable-field character rather than a front-facing portrait sticker. Keep the design much simpler than character art: broad shapes first, `2-3` main color regions, smooth BJD-like face planes, rounded cheeks, tiny mouth/nose, mitten-like hands, tiny planted feet, `1-2` large gold mycelium/spore marks total, and no ornate filigree, scattered cap freckles, particle halo, jewelry clusters, lace-like micro-detail, or oversized eye treatment. Avoid human-with-hat reads, baked shadows, baked backgrounds, text, UI, floor planes, glossy doll-photo rendering, and loose glow effects that hide the body.

If the reference sheet looks good but the final grouped state sheet drifts from it, regenerate the grouped state sheet. If one cell is defective but the grouped sheet is otherwise strong, a targeted single-cell imagegen repair may be used only after explicitly noting why it will not break style consistency. Deterministic post-split processing may clean alpha/chroma fringe, crop, and resize only; it must not alter pose, motion, silhouette, style, or identity. If any split raw frame contains multiple sprites, a border, a background, crop artifacts, missing idle bob, or detail that disappears into noise at mobile scale, regenerate or repair before producing the candidate sheet.
